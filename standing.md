# Game Standing

Scope: active top-level game projects only. Archived projects under `archive/`, shared tooling such as `macroquad-toolkit`, and starter templates are excluded.

Last updated: 2026-08-02

Criteria: ranked by MVP scale and apparent implementation complexity, mainly Rust code size, system count, data/assets, and how much game state the project appears to manage. This is not a fun/polish ranking. LOC/file counts below are a straight recount (`*.rs` under each project's `src/`, plus any sibling crates in the same repo — `mytherra` and `kaiju_sim`) taken fresh this pass, so figures may differ slightly from earlier snapshots even where nothing changed — treat drift within a few percent as noise, not movement. One accounting change since last pass: CODE_STANDARDS now counts only non-test lines against the 800-line file cap (inline `#[cfg(test)]` blocks and extracted `tests.rs` files are exempt), and that accounting is now enforced mechanically — every game carries a `tests/code_standards.rs` gate backed by `macroquad_toolkit::source_gate`, so `cargo test` fails on new violations. Over-limit mentions below use the gate's counts; total LOC figures still include test lines.

**Movement this pass is the opposite of last time — broad.** Every repo has commits since 2026-07-26, but three of them are a docs-consolidation/CI-sync sweep that touched all games identically; the real work concentrated in about a dozen. **Three brand-new games arrived essentially complete within a week of their first commit**: `master_thief` (~19.7k LOC, 347 tests), `daemon_directorate` (~15.5k LOC), and `tb_realms` (~9.6k LOC, a live spectator client for `mytherra`'s world). All three are deliberate text/panel games shipping zero image assets. Two mid-table games roughly doubled — `nightmare_shift` (14.4k → 24.4k) and `nanite_swarm` (8.5k → 20.5k) — and heavy pushes landed on `dragons_hoard` (+8.3k), `dungeon_manager` (+6.3k), `alchemy_tower` (+4.5k), `the_enchanters_ledger` (+4.5k), and `monsterhall` (tower content complete). `stellar_legacy` spent its 39 commits differently: it paid off the structural debt flagged last pass — ten files over the 800-line limit are now zero — while landing a voyage-debrief payoff screen and finishing its economy repricing. Sixteen games are code-unchanged (docs/CI sync only); their entries carry forward verbatim and only shift rank. `mytherra` keeps rank 1 on architectural complexity — it now trails `stellar_legacy` on raw LOC, but nothing else here is a networked live service.

**Finish estimates:** each game carries a rough estimate of how long a full-time team of 4 (mixed engineering / art / design-audio, or generalists managing outsourced assets) would need to take the current codebase to a finished, shippable 1.0 — content-complete, balanced, QA'd, with **all placeholder/procedural artwork replaced by commissioned art and a full sound/music pass**. Estimates are calendar months, dominated as much by asset volume (creature rosters, card illustrations, animation sets) as by remaining code. `dragons_hoard` is a deliberate exception to the art clause — its visuals and music are generated in code by design, not placeholders awaiting replacement — and the three newcomers share that profile: `master_thief` and `daemon_directorate` are text/panel games whose estimates price in portrait/icon sets rather than animation, and `tb_realms`'s own GDD audits its art liability at zero ("the chart is the art"). Treat them all as order-of-magnitude planning numbers, not commitments.
